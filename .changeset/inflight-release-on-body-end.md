Count a request as in-flight until its response body ends rather than until the response headers arrive, so the cap set by `E2B_API_INFLIGHT_REQUESTS` / `E2B_ENVD_INFLIGHT_REQUESTS` / `E2B_ENVD_RPC_INFLIGHT_REQUESTS` matches what the dispatcher is actually holding. Those knobs now bound how many response bodies can be open at once: a streaming command, PTY, watch, or `format: 'stream'` read occupies a slot for its whole lifetime, not just until headers arrive. A slot is handed back when the body is read to its end, cancelled, or errors, and right away for responses that carry no body (a null-body status, `Content-Length: 0`, or a HEAD response). On the envd RPC stack, one slot is reserved for unary/control calls (`commands.kill`, `sendStdin`, `list`, …) so teardown is never queued behind the Connect streams it needs to end — including when the cap is `1`. A request aborted while still waiting for a slot names the concurrency env var rather than advising a larger `requestTimeoutMs`.
